### The Returns to Government R&D: Evidence from US Appropriations Shocks

Andrew J. Fieldhouse, Karel Mertens

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20231684) · [Text read · authors’ November 2024 draft](https://andrewjfieldhouse.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/The_Return_to_Government_R_D_revised_manuscript.pdf)

#### Research Question

How much does federal research funding raise private innovation and productivity? The paper focuses on nondefense R&D, where many benefits may reach firms that did not perform the funded research.

#### Research Method

The authors reconstruct major appropriations changes from budget documents and classify the policy motives. They isolate variation plausibly unrelated to contemporaneous economic conditions, trace dynamic responses, and use semiparametric instrumental variables to estimate the elasticity of business-sector output with respect to publicly financed nondefense R&D capital. The interpretation depends on the validity of the narrative classification and instrument.

#### Research Data

The historical panel covers major US federal R&D agencies in the postwar period. It connects appropriations and research capital to measures of innovation and business-sector productivity. Defense and nondefense funding are tracked separately.

#### Research Findings

Nondefense funding shocks increase measures of innovation and raise long-run business-sector productivity. The authors report implied returns of 140–210% under their production-function specifications and attribute about one-fifth of postwar business-sector total factor productivity growth to government-funded R&D. These estimates are specific to their historical shocks and structural calculation.

#### Contribution

The study separates appropriations surprises from spending that may respond to the economy, then links the surprises to a measure of aggregate returns. It provides a benchmark for a source of research whose spillovers are difficult to recover from the financial returns of individual performers.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Link grants to patent citations and inventor mobility to trace diffusion.
- Estimate separate returns by agency or research field.
- Test whether the response changes with local scientific capacity or the availability of private R&D finance.

### The Unequal Economic Consequences of Carbon Pricing

Diego R. Känzig

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20230448) · [Text read · NBER 31221, September 2025 revision](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31221/w31221.pdf)

#### Research Question

How does tightening carbon pricing affect emissions, aggregate activity and the distribution of household spending losses? The main distinction is between higher direct energy bills and indirect effects on earnings and employment.

#### Research Method

The paper measures unexpected carbon-policy changes from high-frequency allowance-price moves around European Union Emissions Trading System regulatory events. Dynamic estimates trace effects on the economy and on households. The strategy requires those event-window movements to isolate unexpected carbon-policy news from other shocks.

#### Research Data

The analysis combines European carbon and energy market data, aggregate indicators, emissions and innovation measures, and household spending and income evidence. The cross-household analysis compares responses across the income distribution.

#### Research Findings

A restrictive shock raises energy prices and reduces emissions. Green innovation increases while economic activity declines temporarily. Lower-income households cut spending more, partly because their earnings are more exposed. The manuscript attributes roughly two-thirds of the aggregate consumption response to indirect income and employment channels. This is an estimate of responses to the identified shocks.

#### Contribution

The paper brings distributional and macroeconomic responses into the same empirical analysis of carbon pricing. It shows how analyses based only on expenditure shares can miss an important source of household incidence.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Evaluate whether recycling revenues through targeted transfers offsets the earnings channel.
- Link workers to firms and plants to distinguish job loss from wage changes.
- Compare carbon-price incidence in economies with different electricity pricing rules.

### Residential Segregation and Unequal Access to Local Public Services in India: Evidence from 1.5 Million Neighborhoods

Sam Asher, Kritarth Jha, Paul Novosad, Anjali Adukia, Brandon Tan

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20240179) · [Text read · IZA Discussion Paper 18403, February 2026](https://docs.iza.org/dp18403.pdf)

#### Research Question

How segregated are Muslim and Scheduled Caste households in India, and does sorting into neighborhoods coincide with unequal access to public services?

#### Research Method

The authors construct neighborhood-level dissimilarity and isolation measures, then compare service availability within and between cities and rural areas. The comparisons are descriptive. They do not identify how much of the observed pattern comes from discrimination, household preferences or other mechanisms.

#### Research Data

Linked 2011 Population Census, 2012 Socioeconomic and Caste Census, and 2013 Economic Census records cover 1.5 million urban and rural enumeration neighborhoods and about 63% of India's population in the manuscript. Units typically contain around 125 contiguous households. Outcomes include schools, clinics, water, sewerage and electricity.

#### Research Findings

Segregation of both groups is high in international comparisons. Within cities, neighborhoods with higher concentrations of Muslim or Scheduled Caste residents have fewer public services. For urban service allocation, much of the measured disadvantage appears at the within-city neighborhood level. Aggregation to districts or cities can therefore obscure the disparity.

#### Contribution

The paper produces a nationally broad neighborhood-level data set for a setting where local segregation had been difficult to measure. It identifies the geographic scale at which a public-service gap becomes visible.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Use facility openings and neighborhood boundaries to study allocation decisions.
- Link access measures to usage, quality and student or health outcomes.
- Measure changes as urban peripheries expand and households move.

### Durables and the Marginal Propensity to Spend

Nathan Zorzi, Martin Beraja

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20240666) · [Text read · authors’ July 2026 manuscript](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1-JlbGTTZryd_d3INVDw8XdBe9cCdxslE/view)

#### Research Question

How does the inclusion of durable purchases change the size, persistence and distribution of household spending responses to a cash transfer?

#### Research Method

The authors develop a quantitative incomplete-markets model with separate durable and nondurable spending. A smooth adjustment hazard allows the probability of making a lumpy purchase to vary with household circumstances. Simulations compare the resulting transfer response with a rule that scales up observed nondurable spending and with a model of purely nondurable spending.

#### Research Data

The model is disciplined by US evidence on household spending and durable adjustment. The paper uses empirical targets to discipline the model, with results on transfer counterfactuals coming from simulations.

#### Research Findings

Durable purchases represent a substantial share of the marginal propensity to spend. Scaling nondurable responses fits poorly across households, over time and over the business cycle. As check size increases, the total marginal propensity to spend declines more slowly than in a nondurable-only model because households shift some of the additional spending toward durables.

#### Contribution

The analysis distinguishes a spending flow from the services obtained from a durable stock. That distinction matters when translating observed nondurable responses into total spending and when using household responses to evaluate fiscal stimulus.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Link transaction data to vehicle and appliance financing to test the adjustment mechanism.
- Compare transfer responses across local credit conditions.
- Separate earlier replacement from additional durable ownership using panel data.

### Union Bargaining Power and the Amenity-Wage Trade-Off

Lorenzo Lagos

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20240766) · [Text read · IZA Discussion Paper 17034, May 2024](https://docs.iza.org/dp17034.pdf)

#### Research Question

When unions gain bargaining power, do workers get higher wages, better nonwage terms, or both? Does the change in compensation reduce employment?

#### Research Method

The paper uses difference-in-differences around a Brazilian court decision that kept collective-agreement provisions in force after expiration. Differences in agreement coverage provide differential exposure. The author combines wage and employment estimates with a revealed-preference calculation of the monetary value of negotiated amenities.

#### Research Data

The data link the universe of Brazilian collective bargaining agreements, their text and negotiated clauses to establishment and worker outcomes. Contract text makes amenities that are usually unobserved in wage records measurable.

#### Research Findings

More bargaining power increases wages and the number and estimated value of contract amenities. The paper finds no subsequent decline in employment in its design. Amenities make up about 45% of the estimated compensation gain. The interpretation is consistent with unions limiting employer wage-setting power while employers retain discretion over workforce composition.

#### Contribution

The paper shows that bargaining effects on pay can materially understate the gains from negotiated compensation. Observed contract provisions let the author study which nonwage terms matter and compare them with wages.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Compare welfare gains across workers with different caregiving needs.
- Measure compliance with clauses using audits or grievances.
- Test whether results vary by local employer concentration and outside opportunities.

### Inequality and Racial Backlash: Evidence from the Reconstruction Era and the Freedmen's Bureau

Eric Chyn, Kareem Haggag, Bryan A. Stuart

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20241561) · [Text read · NBER 32314, April 2024](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32314/w32314.pdf)

#### Research Question

How did white majorities respond to Reconstruction policies that expanded opportunities and political participation for formerly enslaved Black Americans?

#### Research Method

The authors compare counties with and without Freedmen's Bureau field offices in an event-study design and examine longer-run outcomes. The design uses differential local exposure and pre-period evidence to assess comparability. Long-run correlations require care because policy, migration and local institutions can evolve together.

#### Research Data

The analysis combines historical field-office records, election returns, historical records of racial violence and attacks on schools, and later evidence on organized white supremacist activity and mobility. The key exposure is a local field office in the postwar South.

#### Research Findings

Democratic vote shares initially fell in counties exposed to the Bureau, consistent with early Reconstruction-era political gains. Those gains weakened as oversight ebbed, and measures of white backlash, including racial violence, rose. The manuscript also links exposed counties to subsequent Klan activity and lower intergenerational mobility, with a longer horizon and different evidence than the short-run election estimates.

#### Contribution

The paper studies political and social responses to a major attempt to narrow racial inequality. It shows why evaluating the early effects alone would give an incomplete picture of institutional reform.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Use differences in federal withdrawal timing to study the role of enforcement.
- Link families across censuses to separate migration from local opportunity.
- Examine whether land ownership or Black political organization moderated later violence.

### The Inflation Attention Threshold and Inflation Surges

Oliver Pfäuti

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20250051) · [Text read · author’s March 2026 draft](https://www.oliverpfaeuti.com/website/IAT.pdf)

#### Research Question

Do households update inflation expectations more when inflation is high, and can that change in attention amplify a surge or prolong the return to target?

#### Research Method

The author estimates a threshold in the updating of inflation expectations and compares responses to supply shocks across attention regimes. A New Keynesian model incorporates the estimated change in attention and traces its consequences for expectations and inflation.

#### Research Data

The baseline uses University of Michigan Survey of Consumers inflation expectations from 1978 through 2024. Additional household and firm expectation measures and inflation-related attention indicators provide checks on the baseline interpretation.

#### Research Findings

The estimated threshold is around 4% inflation, with the attention measure approximately doubling above it. Supply shocks have larger and more persistent inflation effects in the high-attention regime. In the model, expectations amplify the rise when attention increases. When attention falls again, elevated prior expectations can slow the final stage of disinflation.

#### Contribution

The paper links three observations through one state-dependent expectations mechanism. The threshold estimate is empirical, while the explanation of the final disinflation stage is a model result.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Estimate thresholds across monetary regimes and countries.
- Test how attention interacts with firms' frequency of price adjustment.
- Compare central-bank communication strategies in a model with state-dependent updating.

### The Causal Effects of Global Supply Chain Disruptions on Macroeconomic Outcomes: Evidence and Theory

Xiwen Bai, Jesús Fernández-Villaverde, Yiliang Li, Francesco Zanetti

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20250081) · [Text read · NBER Working Paper 32098](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32098/w32098.pdf)

#### Research Question

What effect do disruptions between producers and retailers have on inflation and output, and how can they be separated from ordinary demand and supply shocks?

#### Research Method

The paper constructs a congestion index from container ships' movements and develops a search model linking transport congestion to producers' spare capacity. An identified structural VAR uses the model's sign and zero restrictions. The key distinction is that disruptions can leave goods scarce at retail while productive capacity sits idle upstream.

#### Research Data

Automatic Identification System records provide locations, speeds and headings for container ships at 50 major ports in the manuscript. A spatial clustering algorithm distinguishes berths from anchorages. The index is combined with aggregate prices, real activity and capacity measures.

#### Research Findings

Disruption shocks raise prices and reduce activity in the estimated system. The decomposition attributes an important part of 2021 inflation to supply-chain shocks. Conventional demand and supply shocks also account for substantial changes in 2022. The finding that monetary tightening can be more effective after a disruption is conditional on the model and transmission mechanism.

#### Contribution

Measuring congestion directly and using capacity to distinguish shocks connects transport bottlenecks to macroeconomic identification. It helps explain why shortages can coexist with upstream slack.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Validate the congestion index against freight bookings and delivery records.
- Test the capacity restriction at the industry level.
- Model whether alternate ports or inventories dampen the aggregate response.

### Firm Adaptation in Production Networks: Evidence from Extreme Weather Events in Pakistan

Clare Balboni, Johannes Boehm, Mazhar Waseem

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20241027) · [Text read · authors’ November 2025 draft](https://mazharwaseem.com/static/uploads/Pakistan_Floods.pdf)

#### Research Question

Can firms adapt to recurring floods by changing their locations, suppliers and routes, and does this make the wider production network more resilient?

#### Research Method

The paper compares directly and indirectly exposed firms around realized flood events using geographically precise exposure measures. The authors distinguish temporary disruptions from persistent sourcing changes and estimate a spatial model in which firms form trade links under imperfect information about flood risk.

#### Research Data

The manuscript combines Pakistan's monthly formal firm-to-firm tax records for 2011–2018, commercial truck GPS data, satellite flood extents and modeled flood hazards. This separates risk at a firm or supplier's location from risk along the connecting route.

#### Research Findings

Flooding temporarily disrupts production and travel. Exposed firms subsequently tend to move to safer locations or buy from suppliers and along routes with lower hazard. Persistent changes are consistent with updating beliefs about risk. Model counterfactuals imply that these adaptive sourcing decisions meaningfully change the aggregate cost of later floods.

#### Contribution

The study measures private adaptation within a production network. Firms can remain at the same site yet change their exposure by choosing partners and routes, which would be missed by an analysis of facility locations alone.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Test whether publicly supplied flood maps accelerate adaptation before an event.
- Measure spillovers to suppliers that lose buyers.
- Compare the benefits of upgrading roads with insurance or credit for firm relocation.

### Reproducible Aggregation of Sample-Split Statistics

David M. Ritzwoller, Joseph P. Romano

November 2026 issue · Crossref issue date November 1

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20231651) · [Text read · authors’ November 2024 draft](https://davidritzwoller.github.io/files/split.pdf)

#### Research Question

How can researchers control the variability caused by random sample splitting when running the same econometric procedure twice on the same data?

#### Research Method

The paper proposes aggregating statistics across randomly drawn splits using a sequential stopping rule. The analyst sets a discrepancy tolerance and a nominal probability of exceeding it. The rule uses information from repeated splits to determine when the aggregate is stable enough for those choices.

#### Research Data

The evidence combines theoretical analysis, simulations and empirical examples using existing data sets. These examples include cross-validation and other split-dependent econometric statistics. The design studies variability across splits conditional on the underlying data.

#### Research Findings

A single split can yield results that depend substantially on its random assignment. Sequential aggregation improves repeatability, with the nominal error rate approximating the chance that two implementations differ by more than the chosen tolerance under the method's conditions. Tighter tolerances can require more splits. Sampling uncertainty remains a separate issue.

#### Contribution

The method gives the analyst an interpretable reproducibility target instead of an arbitrary fixed number of repeated splits. It applies to procedures in which splitting makes estimation or inference convenient but introduces an extra random component.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Compare split budgets for expensive machine-learning procedures.
- Extend the stopping rule to clustered or dependent splits.
- Evaluate joint reproducibility criteria across many outcomes or tuning parameters.

### Safe Spaces: Shelters or Tribes?

Jean Tirole

Published online ahead of issue · October 27, 2026

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1086/743388) · [Text read · author’s July 2025 manuscript](https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/TSE/documents/doc/by/tirole/safe_spaces_new_form_310725.pdf)

#### Research Question

Why do people segregate into groups of like-minded peers around divisive issues? The paper examines how protection from stigma can coexist with social insulation and intergroup hostility.

#### Research Method

Tirole models individuals who value acting in line with their private values but also care about how an audience perceives them. They may hide behavior in public or join a space that offers a more predictable audience. Equilibria depend on the cost of hiding and on how participation affects opportunities to meet others.

#### Research Data

The paper is theoretical. Examples of contested behaviors and communities motivate the framework. They are not a data set used to estimate it.

#### Research Findings

In the baseline model, low hiding costs can sustain an equilibrium with safe spaces, whereas high costs can lead to transparent public behavior. The model distinguishes sheltering members from stigma from the costs of reducing broader interactions. Welfare effects depend on the comparison and on whether joining a group changes the social graph.

#### Contribution

The framework puts authenticity, reputation and endogenous group formation into a single model. It explains why judging a group's welfare effects requires accounting for both insiders' protection and the interactions that disappear outside it.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Vary anonymity in online communities to separate stigma from intrinsic group preferences.
- Extend the model to overlapping groups with cross-cutting values.
- Use panel network data to study the effect of safe-space access on contacts outside the group.

### Political Parties as Drivers of US Polarization: 1927–2018

Nathan J. Canen, Chad Kendall, Francesco Trebbi

Published online ahead of issue · October 29, 2026

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1086/743333) · [Text read · NBER Working Paper 28296, December 2020](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28296/w28296.pdf)

#### Research Question

How much polarization in congressional roll-call votes reflects legislators' own ideological preferences, and how much reflects control exercised by party leaders?

#### Research Method

The authors fit a multidimensional structural model of voting that allows party leaders to induce members to support or oppose policy. The model imposes structure to separate latent preferences from discipline rather than reading every vote as a direct expression of ideology. The resulting decomposition depends on those identifying assumptions.

#### Research Data

The paper uses US congressional roll-call voting from 1927 through 2018. It examines changes in the distance between the parties and in the estimated influence of leaders over their rank and file.

#### Research Findings

The model attributes a growing share of polarization to party discipline, particularly after the 1970s. The 2020 working version puts the discipline share at around 65% for 2018. Ideological divergence still contributes to the remaining difference. The 65% refers to model-based roll-call polarization, not voter polarization or affective hostility.

#### Contribution

The paper reinterprets a familiar empirical measure of polarization once organized parties are allowed to influence observed votes. The mechanism suggests leadership and legislative procedure can matter even if preferences are slow to change.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Validate estimated discipline against records of actual whip campaigns.
- Compare voting across procedural rules that constrain leadership.
- Link preference estimates to candidate entry and fundraising to separate selection from leadership influence.

### A Theory of Redistribution and Public Good Provision under Nondemocracy

Nicola Persico

Published online ahead of issue · October 30, 2026

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1086/743332)

**Abstract-based note.** Two politicians compete for office by making promises in a setting where political support is observable and citizens can face retaliation for backing a loser. In equilibrium, citizens back the expected winner, giving groups with greater coordination capacity an advantage in attracting resources. The model changes the incentives for both redistribution and public-good provision relative to voting models and gives no voluntary transition to democracy under its assumptions.

## The Quarterly Journal of Economics

### The Effects of School Phone Bans: National Evidence from Lockable Pouches

Hunt Allcott, Jason Baron, Thomas Dee, Angela L Duckworth, Matthew Gentzkow, Brian Jacob

Published online ahead of issue · October 30, 2026

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjag050) · [Text read · authors’ August 2026 manuscript](https://web.stanford.edu/~gentzkow/research/PhoneBans.pdf)

#### Research Question

What happens when schools enforce phone restrictions using lockable pouches? The paper looks at actual phone access, academic performance and possible costs of enforcement.

#### Research Method

The authors use a staggered difference-in-differences design built around adoption timing. Sales records identify implementation, and independently measured phone activity checks whether the policy actually reduces access. The comparison relies on credible counterfactual trends among comparison schools. Some self-reported outcomes have differential pre-trends that limit interpretation.

#### Research Data

Nationwide data combine pouch sales, teacher surveys, mobile-device GPS activity, standardized tests and administrative school records. The authors distinguish middle schools and high schools and track outcomes across years since adoption.

#### Research Findings

Phone use falls materially. In the first year, disciplinary incidents rise and there is suggestive evidence that student well-being declines. Discipline effects fade and later well-being estimates improve. Average test effects are near zero, with modest gains in high school, particularly in math, and small losses in middle school. The paper finds little effect on attendance and no clear improvements in classroom attention or perceived online bullying.

#### Contribution

The study tests a specific, verifiably enforced restriction at national scale. It distinguishes changes in phone use from learning gains and documents differences by grade level and time since implementation.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Randomize implementation support to test whether it reduces early discipline costs.
- Compare pouches with simpler storage policies.
- Follow students across school and home to measure substitution of screen time.

## The Review of Economic Studies

### A Model of Enclosures: Coordination, Conflict, and Efficiency in the Transformation of Land Property Rights

Matthew J Baker, Jonathan Conning

Published online ahead of issue · October 30, 2026

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdag118) · [Text read · authors’ January 2025 arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.01592)

#### Research Question

When does decentralized conversion of open-access or customary land into private property improve efficiency, and when does it lead to excessive or insufficient enclosure?

#### Research Method

The authors use an aggregative game in which an individual's enclosure incentive depends on the aggregate enclosure decision. They vary the cost and productivity of private use, population pressure and the rules governing the commons. Comparing equilibrium to feasible welfare benchmarks reveals how private incentives differ from social returns.

#### Research Data

There is no original empirical analysis. Historical enclosure and contemporary formalization provide applications. Numerical examples illustrate the equilibrium mechanisms rather than estimating them.

#### Research Findings

Changes in costs, productivity or population can produce smooth or abrupt shifts in property regimes. Outcomes include coordination failures that prevent beneficial enclosure and property races that enclose too much. Strengthening commons governance or compensating displaced users can help, but correcting one distortion in isolation can worsen outcomes in the presence of others.

#### Contribution

The model connects productivity-based, institutional and distributional accounts of land transformation. It provides a common way to separate rents redistributed by enclosure from gains created by it.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Combine digitized title records with historical maps to test for abrupt transitions.
- Add bargaining among customary users with unequal rights.
- Compare policy packages that change registration costs and compensation together.

### Conversations at Scale: Robust AI-Led Interviews

Friedrich Geiecke, Xavier Jaravel

Published online ahead of issue · October 27, 2026

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdag114) · [Text read · authors’ October 2025 manuscript](https://www.xavierjaravel.com/_files/ugd/bacd2d_0c3186cfe6e445b68001f56c2d9b0fe5.pdf)

#### Research Question

Can researchers conduct open-ended qualitative interviews at scale with AI while retaining respondent trust, depth and robustness?

#### Research Method

The paper develops a configurable platform for LLM-led interviews, including voice. It compares interview modes and evaluates performance using participant ratings and expert judgments. Tests examine the fidelity of summaries and whether AI probing yields information beyond a static free-text response.

#### Research Data

The study uses original interview and evaluation data from applications covering decision making, political views, subjective mental states and respondents' mental models of policy. It combines ratings by respondents with independent assessment by human experts.

#### Research Findings

The authors report favorable evaluations across these settings. The platform can gather rich responses at a scale that would be costly with expert interviews alone. The evidence concerns the configurations and participant samples tested. Changing models, language or topic may change the results, and standardized prompts do not remove all concerns about interpretation.

#### Contribution

The paper provides a practical empirical method and a validation strategy, linking the flexibility of follow-up questions to the reach of surveys. Its comparisons help separate quality of interview interaction from quality of downstream synthesis.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Audit answer quality across accents and languages.
- Randomize follow-up policies to identify which probes add information.
- Compare reproducibility under pinned open models versus proprietary systems across software updates.

### Price Gouging and the Monopoly Option

Samuel Häfner, Curtis R Taylor

Published online ahead of issue · October 26, 2026

[Publication or listing record](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdag119)

**Abstract-based note.** In a dynamic oligopoly, a supply interruption forces firms to sell out of inventories. A seller can price high and forgo current sales in the hope of becoming the last firm with stock. This option can make prices jump above the competitive path at the onset of the disruption and stay elevated during it. The mechanism is a theoretical prediction about strategic inventory depletion, rather than an empirical estimate of the prevalence of price gouging.

### From Design to Disclosure

S. Nageeb Ali, Andreas Kleiner, Kun Zhang

Forthcoming · listed November 2, 2026

[Publication or listing record](https://www.restud.com/accepted-papers/) · [Text read · authors’ July 2026 manuscript](https://kunzhang.org/file/akz.pdf)

#### Research Question

When a sender can disclose hard information voluntarily but cannot commit to a disclosure policy, can she obtain the payoffs available under committed information design?

#### Research Method

The model has an informed sender with verifiable evidence and a receiver who can adjust allocations and transfers. The key condition is that full revelation is worst for the sender because the receiver can extract her surplus. The equivalence also uses a worst-case-type condition for messages and continuity of receiver payoffs. The authors compare disclosure-game equilibria with feasible commitment payoffs.

#### Research Data

This is a theoretical paper. Monopoly pricing, insurance and policy bargaining motivate the model and illustrate its assumptions. No original empirical data are analyzed.

#### Research Findings

Under the stated conditions, disclosure equilibrium payoffs can approximate the payoffs achievable by information design, and the result characterizes the relevant payoff sets. In particular, some equilibria deliver approximately the sender's commitment payoff. The result is about existence and approximation across equilibria, so it leaves open which equilibrium actual participants select.

#### Contribution

The main evidence is quantitative calibration and simulation, anchored to a US 2025 benchmark including a 23% saving rate. The paths are model scenarios rather than observed responses to an automation shock.

#### Research Findings

Under the benchmark technology path, optimizing saving delivers 11.7% annual growth versus 2.2% under the fixed rate. More income accrues to capital, which raises aggregate saving without requiring a large increase in the fraction of capital income reinvested. The simulated ten-year real yield rises from 5.1% to 13.6% on impact. Households unable to save can lose substantial consumption-equivalent welfare even as output grows.

#### Contribution

The paper distinguishes a technological bottleneck from a capital-supply bottleneck. That distinction changes how to read early data on investment, wage growth and long yields during a transition.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Allow international capital flows and risk premia.
- Estimate task substitution from firm-level adoption.
- Test fiscal transfers that protect hand-to-mouth households while preserving investment incentives.

### Affirmative Action, Faculty Productivity and Caste Interactions: Evidence from Engineering Colleges in India

Robert W. Fairlie, Saurabh Khanna, Prashant Loyalka, Gagandeep S. Sachdeva

NBER Working Paper 35963 · November 2 batch

[Publication or listing record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35963) · [Text read · NBER manuscript](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35963/w35963.pdf)

#### Research Question

Do faculty hired under affirmative-action reservations in Indian engineering colleges differ in instructional or research productivity, and do students benefit from having a teacher in their own reservation category?

#### Research Method

The paper combines national sampling with a subset of colleges that assign students to classrooms randomly. The classroom variation supports teaching comparisons without relying solely on student choice. The authors also estimate teacher–student category interactions. Research and service comparisons use observed faculty outcomes rather than random assignment of faculty hiring status.

#### Research Data

The sample covers 50 Indian engineering and technology colleges. Data on faculty training, rank and experience are linked to course grades, tests, follow-on courses, attendance, dropout, graduation and other outcomes. The authors also measure research and administrative activity.

#### Research Findings

Reservation-category faculty have lower measured credentials and experience on average. The authors find little difference across many measures of instruction. Immediate course grades are slightly higher in classes taught by reservation-category faculty. The matching estimates do not reject a zero teacher-like-me effect, and research and service estimates are small and imprecise.

#### Contribution

The paper distinguishes observable credentials from realized productivity and tests several channels relevant to evaluating hiring quotas. Its classroom design makes the teaching comparison more informative than a comparison of faculty CVs.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Measure longer-run earnings and employment after college.
- Test whether results vary with mentorship and departmental support.
- Study how different reservation thresholds affect the pool of faculty applicants and hires.

### Unraveling the Cobweb of Global Imbalances: Drivers, Vulnerabilities and Adjustment Scenarios

Stefan Avdjiev, Kristin Forbes, Tsvetelina Nenova, Marjorie Santos

NBER Working Paper 35962 · November 2 batch

[Publication or listing record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35962) · [Text read · NBER manuscript](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35962/w35962.pdf)

#### Research Question

Why have countries' net international investment positions diverged, and how would changes in asset prices, exchange rates or trade propagate through external balance sheets?

#### Research Method

The authors decompose changes in net foreign positions into proximate drivers including trade, investment income and valuation changes. They combine exposures with stylized adjustment scenarios. The exercises quantify accounting and financial spillovers under the scenarios rather than identifying a structural causal response to an observed policy intervention.

#### Research Data

The data cover 28 economies and draw on international investment positions, asset and liability compositions, cross-border investment income and market price, exchange-rate and interest-rate series. The unit is primarily the country's external balance sheet over time.

#### Research Findings

The US net position deteriorated as most other major economies improved. Valuations and investment income dominate changes at short and longer horizons in the decomposition, though trade remains relevant, especially over long horizons. Simulations of dollar depreciation, equity repricing, higher interest rates and smaller trade gaps have sharply different country effects.

#### Contribution

The paper shifts the analysis from annual trade or current-account flows to stocks and their exposures. It shows why reducing a trade imbalance alone may have a limited effect on the accumulated international balance sheet.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Trace losses through bilateral and sectoral exposures.
- Incorporate exchange-rate hedges and derivatives.
- Allow portfolio rebalancing and policy responses in a dynamic version of the scenarios.

### Can Home Visits Re-Engage Chronically Absent Students? Evidence from the LEAP Program

Eric Brunner, Shangyue Jiang, Stephen Ross

NBER Working Paper 35961 · November 2 batch

[Publication or listing record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35961) · [Text read · NBER manuscript](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35961/w35961.pdf)

#### Research Question

Can a home visit by a teacher or other school employee help chronically absent students reconnect with school after the pandemic? The authors ask about attendance, behavior and academic outcomes.

#### Research Method

Event studies and difference-in-differences use staggered visit timing and compare students within grades and schools. The authors examine how observed characteristics and unobserved selection into timing affect the estimated trajectory. The identifying variation comes from when a student received a visit, rather than randomized assignment.

#### Research Data

Student-level Connecticut administrative records cover the 2021–22 to 2023–24 school years. The program operated in 15 disadvantaged districts. Outcomes include attendance, chronic absenteeism, whether there was a reported disciplinary incident and academic performance.

#### Research Findings

The estimates imply a 4–5 percentage point gain in attendance and a 13–15 point drop in chronic absenteeism, with a 3–5 point decline in the probability of a disciplinary incident. Estimates about five months after treatment are reported in the conclusion. An apparent buildup in effects largely disappears after addressing selection in timing. The study finds no detectable same-year achievement effect.

#### Contribution

The study evaluates direct family outreach as a response to persistent disengagement and shows why adjusting for who receives treatment when matters for interpreting event-study dynamics.

#### Potential Extensions

*Proposed research directions.*

- Randomize repeated versus one-off visits to measure persistence.
- Compare teachers, counselors and trained visitors as providers.
- Link longer-run achievement and graduation to treatment records to test whether attendance gains translate into learning.
